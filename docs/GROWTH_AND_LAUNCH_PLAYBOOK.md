# NFT growth and launch playbook

## Why this system is selective
The 2026 market is not a reliable "mint anything and go viral" market. The Block's 2026 outlook describes a selective market where collections with real products, IP or communities retain attention while much of the long tail struggles. Art Basel/UBS reporting also indicates renewed collector interest in digital art, but that does not imply demand for any particular new collection. Treat this as a reason to build a distinct artistic identity and collector relationships, not as a sales forecast.

## What the factory prepares
- A bilingual English/Persian campaign draft for each artwork that has explicit owner approval and rights confirmation.
- A 14-day pre/post release content calendar with varied story, process, detail, education and community formats.
- SEO title, meta description and keywords.
- A simple CSV tracker for qualified visits, listing clicks, saves, substantive replies, collector questions, commissions, completed sales and net receipts.
- Human review checkpoints. It never posts, emails, DMs, mints, lists or spends.

## Release sequence
1. Research demand using completed sales and evidence-backed signals; ignore floor price as proof of sales.
2. Build one cohesive, recognizable collection with a clear curatorial thesis.
3. Prepare a sample gallery and an honest project page: artist identity, concept, supply, chain, schedule, fees, royalties, risks and FAQ.
4. Build genuine interest before release through thoughtful participation and personalized outreach. No purchased followers, fake volume, wash trading, spam or false scarcity.
5. Use a small first release matched to demonstrated audience size. Do not choose a large supply or high price based on wishful thinking.
6. Publish only after the actual listing URL, price, chain, supply and fee preview are verified.
7. Review qualified engagement and verified net receipts. Iterate the story, format or offer; never manufacture activity.

## Marketplace-specific notes
OpenSea says its featured drops evaluation considers art quality, content, community following and engagement; selection is not guaranteed and independent creators cannot simply pay or request placement. A drop page can present the project name, creator links, schedule, sample gallery, roadmap and team information. For a creator without an established community, OpenSea currently recommends considering an open collection rather than assuming a timed drop is suitable.

objkt's creator guide recommends sharing work on social channels, engaging collectors, joining its Discord, collaborating and keeping the artist profile current. These are community-building suggestions, not guarantees of reach or sales.

## KPI hierarchy
Primary: verified net wallet receipts after marketplace/network fees and refunds.
Leading indicators: qualified listing visits, saves/watchlist additions, substantive collector replies, commission inquiries.
Vanity metrics: raw impressions, likes and follower count. Track them, but do not optimize the project around them.

## Important
No strategy can guarantee trending status, sales, or a specific revenue amount. Never promise investment returns. Verify platform availability, fees, local law, wallet provider access and tax obligations before a real release. Never share seed phrases or private keys.
